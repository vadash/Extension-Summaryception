# 0024 — One Route Series owns one Narrative Chain hop

One module (`src/core/request-series.js`) owns one connection route's retry series behind one interface: `runRouteSeries({ prompt, repairPrompt, signal, profile, route, routeLabel, maxRetries, notify })` returns a Route Series Result and only classifies provider throws — it never surfaces raw exception objects to its caller. It replaces `RequestRunner.runAttemptSeries` and its two prepared-attempt helpers, and deletes `src/core/request-attempt.js`; the runner shrinks to what routing actually owns: route walking, the health bucket, the route-cycle wait, and the Run Outcome verdicts. ADR-0019 asked for a series seam at this level; this document names it and restates the request path's vocabulary around it.

**The attempt protocol collapses into the attempt vocabulary.** The old attempt result was a triple-encoded bag: a live `settings` read, then `success`/`aborted`/`shouldRetry`/`hardFailover` flags, then a `failureStatus` string the log layer re-mapped to a transaction label, plus a dead `layerRepair`/`repairFeedback` pair in the 13-field params bag. The attempt now returns one `AttemptResult` — `completed`, `aborted`, `hard-failover`, `failed{retryable}`, `rejected{reason}`, or `guard-stopped` — and the transaction log maps exactly those statuses, so the log label and the protocol can no longer drift apart. The series result is the same six statuses at series scope (`RouteSeriesResult`); only `rejected` drops the repair payloads, which nothing above the repair switch reads.

**Exhaustion is a property of the ending, not a recomputed flag.** The old callers re-derived "retries ran out" three ways: `retriesExhausted` built from `shouldRetry && attempt >= maxRetries`, an attempts accumulator that counted only `failed` hops, and a `retryable || hardFailover` walk condition. A Route Series Result encodes it once: `failed{retryable}` and `rejected` are surfaced exclusively after the budget ran out, `hard-failover` after it was skipped, `guard-stopped` and non-retryable `failed` as bare endings. The runner's walk (`shouldTryNextRoute`, the primary-exhausted bucket, the terminal classification) reads that property instead of rebuilding it from flags.

**Stop reasons keep their rule, lose their coupling.** `getRetryStopReason` and `shouldSwitchToRepairPrompt` move their predicates onto the attempt status (`status === 'rejected'` replaces the four-name validation-status list); the rule is unchanged — validation failures switch to the repair prompt while budget remains, everything else settles. `classifyAttemptRetryStatus` keeps its three-way classification and drops the `failureStatus` echo; the series maps it to statuses in one place (`classifyAttemptError`).

**Tests drive the series through the provider seam.** The runner outcomes tests mocked `runSingleAttempt` — the real bugs (how attempts are composed, what the guard emits mid-run, where retry waits surface) hid above the mock. The reworked suite mocks only `sendSummarizerRequest`, so every outcome, notify event, and call count is the real Output Hygiene and retry policy at work; the attempt-level timeout and notify suites retarget onto the series. One documented event delta: the runner's guard-block outcome no longer sees an empty recorder — the attempt layer's structured `easy-guard-blocked` event now reaches the notify adapter, which is what ADR-0019 already specified.

Consequences: `request-attempt.js` is deleted and `request-series.js` takes its place; `CONTEXT.md` gains the Route Series term beside Narrative Chain; `tests/request-series*.test.js` replace the `request-attempt-*.test.js` files; `request-runner-outcomes.test.js` drives the provider seam. ADR-0008 (profile frozen at dispatch), ADR-0019 (outcome vocabulary and notify events), and ADR-0023 (retry resolution at dispatch) are unchanged — this document decides who owns one hop, not what a hop means.
